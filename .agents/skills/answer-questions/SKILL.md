# Objective

You are responding to one question about the plan/research below. Your job is to answer it verifiably, not to generate more questions — but you should use whatever mix of evidence, reasoning, and judgment produces the most honest answer. The evidence-based standard is the default, not the cage.

The answer should target whatever the question challenges: a stated assumption, a load-bearing claim, missing information, vague scope, a ruled-out alternative, or a failure signal.
It should quote the exact question it answers, and where possible it grounds claims in evidence — a citation, an observation, a definition, a measured number, a prior verified result, or an explicit concession that no such basis exists.

An answer should cover:

- The question being answered, restated precisely so the response cannot drift
- The evidence (or reasoning) that supports the answer, named specifically
- The basis for it — why it is trustworthy, what limits its trustworthiness, or where the evidence runs out and judgment begins
- What the answer does *not* cover, so the reader sees its true shape
- The leading alternative explanations, and why they are weaker (or where they are not, on the available evidence)
- Where the answer would change: what new evidence, observation, or calculation would falsify it

Guidance:

- Be specific to the question asked. Generic answers that could apply to any plan are usually a sign the response has drifted.
- Quote the question where it helps; skip the quote when the question is long and the target is obvious.
- One answer per question is preferred, though a compound answer is acceptable when a question genuinely braids together.
- You should propose solutions, summarise the plan back where it clarifies the answer, and commit to your reasoning.
- Where evidence is available, ground your claim in it and name the basis. Where it isn't, say so — and proceed by reasoning or judgment, labelled as such.
- You should distinguish *known* (evidence cited), *inferred* (reasoning with the gap named), and *assumed* (stated as such).

This skill addresses one question per response. A complete treatment of the full set of challenges will require multiple answers — one per question — each applying whatever mix of evidence and judgment fits that particular question. Stop after answering the single question in front of you; the remaining questions are a separate, equally honest exercise.

Use sub-agents if they are available to you, using one per question - unless there is just one question.
